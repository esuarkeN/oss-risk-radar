\chapter{Practical Demonstrator}
\label{chap:demonstrator}

This chapter applies OSS Risk Radar to repositories outside the foundation seed.
It serves the Design Science Research demonstration phase and provides an
out-of-distribution error analysis
\cite{hevner2004design,peffers2007design}. The application can expose failure
modes not visible in a test set drawn from the seed distribution.

The input is a real project's dependency set. Scores come from the promoted
artifact bundle (Section~\ref{sec:impl-deployment}). For a shared dataset hash
and regime, the backend scores each staged model evaluated in
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} and averages the outputs into an
\emph{ensemble profile} (Appendix~\ref{sec:impl-backend}). Every reported score
is the mean of two models; their spread becomes the model-agreement factor.

Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} evaluates individual artifacts, not this ensemble.
Comparison with its strongest member,
\texttt{xgboost\sdash full\sdash history}, remains future work
(Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}). Furthermore, 29 of 30 repositories use the
weaker cold-start regime. This coverage limitation motivates the cache-expansion
work in Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}. The chapter therefore analyzes the deployed
configuration rather than measuring predictive accuracy again. Outputs reflect
public repository state on \texttt{2026-07-09}.

\section{Example Repository Set}
\label{sec:demo-mapping}

Each repository is scored \emph{individually}, and
software-composition-analysis tooling supplies the external inventory
\cite{ortAnalyzer} (Section~\ref{sec:impl-architecture}).

The example is \texttt{ArchipelagoMW\sslash Archipelago}, a large, active
open-source multi-world game randomizer. Its declared Python dependencies map to
30 source repositories, from \texttt{flask}, \texttt{jinja2}, and
\texttt{werkzeug} to smaller packages. Dependency resolution and graph
construction are outside the artifact. Each declared repository is scored
independently, and the demonstrator orders the 30 scores without modeling
relationships between dependencies.\kiref{Claude Opus 5/4} This mixture was
chosen to avoid a uniformly low-risk example.

The workflow enriches live metadata and applies the staged ensemble. Only
\texttt{flask} is present in the feature cache and receives full-history scoring;
the other 29 repositories use cold-start
(Section~\ref{sec:method-prediction-regimes}). This shows the coverage limit for
arbitrary submissions. Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} shows that on
active-at-\(t\) repositories, full-history exceeds
cold-start by \(0.030\) ROC-AUC
(Section~\ref{sec:results-history-ablation}), and most example repositories are
active. Cold-start metrics also come from the seed distribution, while this
example contains arbitrary submissions.

\section{Risk Ranking Output and Interpretation Guidance}
\label{sec:demo-ranking}

Each result contains an inactivity-risk score (0--100), an action level
(\texttt{monitor}, \texttt{review}, or \texttt{replace\_candidate}), prediction
regime, and evidence support. Evidence support combines signal completeness,
in-distribution fit, and calibration support
(Appendix~\ref{sec:impl-scoring-service}). A separate field contains the
OpenSSF-derived security-posture score, which the inactivity model does not read
(Section~\ref{sec:method-research-design}).
Figure~\ref{fig:demo-distribution} shows risk buckets and action levels. They use
separate thresholds on the same score. The review cut is \(\geq 50\), below the
\(\geq 60\) high-risk cut, so one medium-risk repository is raised for review.
Six of 30 repositories exceed \texttt{monitor}. Table~\ref{tab:demo-ranking}
lists these repositories and the full-history item; Appendix~\ref{appendix:demo-full}
gives all 30. Every score is distinct because
Section~\ref{sec:impl-models} replaces flat bin lookup with interpolation.

\paragraph{Comparison of inactivity and security posture.} Inactivity and security posture are
computed separately and shown without a combined score. \texttt{bsdiff4} has
inactivity risk \(92.1\) and posture \(6\); \texttt{flask} has risk \(9.5\) and
posture \(77\). In the middle, \texttt{jinja2} and \texttt{markupsafe} receive
risks \(77.1\) and \(74.2\), but posture scores \(58\) and \(54\). They are
mature Pallets projects with branch protection and review requirements whose
maintenance has slowed. \texttt{cymem} combines risk \(74.9\) with posture
\(24\). The two columns distinguish quieter, well-governed projects from one
that is both quiet and weakly governed.

This presentation provides no combination rule or fitted weighting, and whether
the two columns improve analyst decisions remains to be tested in the user study
proposed in Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}.

\paragraph{Highest-ranked repository.} \texttt{bsdiff4}
(\texttt{ilanschnell\sslash bsdiff4}) is a small binary-diff library with risk
\(92.1\). It has low popularity, one maintainer, and no detected release cadence.
The cold-start score has evidence support \(0.69\). These caveats make the
repository a candidate for manual review, not a judgement of abandonment
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:demo-full}).

\paragraph{Construct-validity case: Jinja2 and MarkupSafe.}
\texttt{jinja2} (\texttt{pallets/jinja}, \(77.1\)) and
\texttt{markupsafe} (\texttt{pallets/markupsafe}, \(74.2\)) are widely used
Pallets projects, making their \texttt{review} status initially appear erroneous.
At the run date, their last pushes were \texttt{2025-06-14} and
\texttt{2025-09-27}. The inactivity label measures the absence of public
maintenance signals (Section~\ref{sec:method-definitions}), so these scores match
the target. No twelve-month outcome is yet available to test them because their
windows do not close until 2027. Both have \(100\%\) signal completeness,
excluding imputation as the cause.

A stable, feature-complete library can resemble a project in decline: both show
infrequent commits, long release gaps, and few active contributors. Public
metadata cannot distinguish low activity from low \emph{need} for activity
(Section~\ref{sec:background-metric-pitfalls}). The author's inspection found no
maintenance concern requiring action in either case, although reviewer decisions
were not tested. The scores are consistent with the operational label but not
with this manual assessment. They illustrate the construct-validity threat and
the need for human review
(Section~\ref{sec:validity-construct}).\kiref{Claude Opus 5/8}

\paragraph{Low-risk comparison.}
\texttt{flask} (\texttt{pallets/flask}) receives risk \(9.5\) with evidence
support \(0.91\). As the only full-history repository, it has complete coverage
and no imputation; its ensemble members predict \(5.4\%\) and \(13.5\%\)
inactivity risk.

The difference from \texttt{jinja2} and \texttt{markupsafe} is not caused by
regime alone. \texttt{flask} was last pushed on \texttt{2026-06-10}, one month
before the run, while the other gaps were thirteen and nine months. Regime and
activity are confounded, so this example does not isolate the value of history;
Section~\ref{sec:results-history-ablation} does. Evidence support states how much
repository information was available beyond the risk score.\kiref{Claude Opus 5/8}

The score is intended to rank repositories for review; it does not certify
security or identify a known defect. Low evidence support and imputation reduce
confidence. The cold-start model uses fewer features and is calibrated against
the sampled inactive rate of \(0.661\), which exceeds that of a real dependency
population (Section~\ref{sec:method-limitations}). Its absolute probabilities are
therefore overstated until recalibrated for the target population, although the
ranking remains useful for triage.

The ranking raises six of the 30 repositories above the monitor level, with
\texttt{flask} as the full-history reference. Manual inspection found no
maintenance concern for the two
\texttt{review} items \texttt{jinja2} and \texttt{markupsafe}, showing why
review remains necessary. Whether the ordering improves analyst throughput or
decision quality requires a controlled comparison with unranked triage
(Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}).
